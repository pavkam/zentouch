ZenTouch Help

Tap to click, drag with one finger, and scroll with two. ZenTouch runs in the menu bar, so closing Settings keeps touch input working.

Supported monitors

ASUS ZenScreen Touch MB16AMTR is the supported model today, tested with ZenTouch on macOS 27. Keep its display rotation at 0°. Other ASUS touchscreens need compatibility testing before we can support them; see the full model list.

First run

  1. Connect the ZenScreen's USB data cable. A video-only connection won't send touches to your Mac.
  2. Click the ZenTouch menu bar icon and choose Settings….
  3. In App, use the permission buttons to allow Input Monitoring and Accessibility in System Settings. These let ZenTouch read the touchscreen and send clicks and gestures. Quit and reopen the app if macOS asks you to. Green checks show when the permissions are ready.
  4. In Touch, select your ZenScreen and enable Active.

Your fingers should appear in the Settings preview. Taps click where you touch, and by default the mouse pointer moves to your touch position.

Gestures

GestureResult
One-finger tapClick
Move one finger while touchingDrag
Move two fingers togetherScroll up, down, left or right
Two-finger tap, or hold one finger still and liftRight-click
Three-finger swipe up, then liftMission Control
Three-finger swipe down, then liftApp Exposé; still needs live testing
Three-finger swipe left or rightSwitch desktops or full-screen apps; still needs live testing
Two-finger pinchZoom in or out in apps that support pinch

Three-finger swipes are on by default. Place three fingers, move them together, then lift. Up and down act when you lift; the view doesn't follow your fingers. Three-finger dragging isn't supported.

Settings has three tabs: Touch, Gestures and App. Its fixed window shows every gesture without scrolling.

Open Settings → Gestures for animated examples and separate switches for Click & drag, Scroll, Pinch to zoom, Switch desktops, Mission Control and App Exposé. Click & drag also controls right-clicks.

Change any switch while input is running, then lift all your fingers before trying the new setting. Choices are saved across quitting and reconnecting. Fresh installs enable all gestures; updates keep earlier pinch and three-finger opt-outs. These options don't change your Mac's trackpad settings.

Spread two fingers to zoom in, or pinch them together to zoom out. The receiving app must support pinch zoom. If nothing happens, check the switch and try Preview or another zoomable view. Pinch works with both pointer modes. Gesture previews pause when hidden and become still illustrations with macOS Reduce Motion.

Keep the mouse pointer where it is

Stop input, enable Keep pointer stationary in Settings → Touch, then start again. Taps, dragging and scrolling go to the window under your finger while the mouse stays put. Your choice is saved and used after reconnects.

Tapping and scrolling have been confirmed in live use on macOS 27. Compatibility still varies by app and control. Desktop, menu bar and some menu controls may not respond. If a window closes during a drag, remaining events are dropped; the mode never falls back to moving the pointer. Turn the option off to restore normal pointer behavior.

Start, stop and reconnect

The menu's Active checkmark is your on/off switch. Settings has the same Active checkbox. Closing Settings keeps touch working; Quit ZenTouch stops it and exits.

If the screen sleeps or disconnects, ZenTouch waits for it to return. Active stays checked so input can resume without restarting. A slashed menu bar icon means the selected screen or its USB touch connection is missing. Open Settings or hover over the icon to see what's missing. Touch controls become available when the hardware returns. You can always stop input to cancel automatic resuming.

Launch when you sign in

Enable Launch at login in Settings → App. ZenTouch starts in the menu bar at your next login and resumes touch input if it was previously active. You can change this with the monitor disconnected. If macOS needs approval, the checkbox shows a dash; click Open Login Items and approve ZenTouch. Changes in System Settings are reflected in the checkbox.

See your touches

Enable Show touch indicators in Settings → Touch for teal rings that follow your fingers on the screen, including with Settings closed. You can toggle this while input is running. Rings disappear when you lift or stop input. They're off by default; macOS Reduce Motion makes them steady instead of pulsing.

If something doesn't work

  • No fingers in the preview: check the USB data cable, Input Monitoring permission, and that touch input is running.
  • Fingers appear, but nothing clicks: check Accessibility permission and the selected display.
  • ZenTouch isn't in Privacy & Security: use + to add the installed app, then enable it.
  • Active is disabled or the icon is slashed: make sure the selected screen is awake and its USB data cable is connected. Settings explains which connection is missing.
  • The pointer doesn't line up: stop input, select the correct display, and check that its rotation is 0°.
  • A control ignores stationary-pointer touch: stop input, turn the option off and start again. Include the app and control in a bug report.
  • Touch stops after a forced quit: reconnect the USB cable. A normal Stop or Quit lets ZenTouch restore the controller's previous mode.

Open Logs Folder opens ~/Library/Logs/ZenTouch. Logs rotate automatically and use at most 24 MiB. They include touchscreen positions and actions, so review excerpts before sharing them. For help, open an issue with your monitor model, macOS version and what you tried.

What still needs testing

Clicks, two-finger scrolling, three-finger Mission Control, use with Settings closed, and touch recovery after a monitor power cycle have been tested on an MB16AMTR with macOS 27. Desktop switching and App Exposé still need live testing. Native AppKit pinch delivery has passed a separate-process zoom-in, zoom-out and cancellation test with the pointer stationary; physical pinch and independent switches are confirmed in live use. Wider app compatibility remains under test. The latest attach/detach icon and control changes have automated coverage; physical testing is pending.

ZenTouch sends mouse and scroll events; it doesn't add direct-touch support to Mac apps or provide every Apple trackpad gesture. Stationary-pointer routing, three-finger gestures and pinch rely on undocumented macOS behavior and may change with OS updates.
